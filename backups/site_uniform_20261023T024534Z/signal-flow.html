How a signal is generated

Deterministic, read-only evaluation on completed 5-minute candles. No lookahead, one trade per instrument per day. See methodology and CPR guide.

Why each gate exists

  • Session gate. Evaluating only on weekdays inside 09:15–15:30 IST keeps every observation comparable — weekend and holiday price action has different microstructure and would pollute the journal. Fridays are still evaluated: a Friday base BEAR setup is skipped (measured drag on the short book) while a Friday upside breakout add-on is allowed.
  • Data gates. Requiring a completed candle plus a fresh feed prevents the two classic backtest illusions: acting on prices that did not exist yet, and acting on stale quotes. Anything failing here becomes WAIT (retry on the next candle), never a fabricated fill.
  • Day levels. Computing TC/BC/Pivot/R1/R2/S1/S2/PDH/PDL once from the previous session freezes the map — the levels cannot drift mid-day to flatter the outcome.
  • Trigger scan, base bearish + breakout add-on. The base book evaluates only the bearish trigger family; a breakout add-on fills a session only when no base setup fires (long above resistance / short below support). A fixed evaluation order with first-trigger-wins removes discretion about "which signal counts" and guarantees at most one observation per instrument per day.
  • Entry gate. Pinning entry to the next candle's open (inside 09:20–15:20) with fixed 0.5% stop / 2% target bounds makes every journal row reproducible from the same inputs — and identical to what the backtest records for the same trigger.

Reading WAIT vs NO TRADE

WAIT is provisional: the session is alive but no gate has produced an observation yet (no trigger, late read, momentary data gap). The scanner keeps evaluating each new 5-minute candle. NO TRADE is terminal for the day: a gate that cannot resolve before the close (holiday, session end with no trigger, persistent data failure). Days with no classified session are normal — particularly inside wide CPR rotation — and are recorded as such in the observations journal rather than hidden.

How to read a published alert

  • Direction + level (e.g. NIFTY BEAR R1) tells you which rule fired and where — look the rule up in the trigger table above.
  • Signal time is the trigger candle; entry time is the next candle's open — the same entry the backtest records for that trigger. Alerts follow within about a minute of the entry candle.
  • Frozen trade: once fired, entry/stop/target never move for that session row — the journal studies the rule's outcome, not a managed position.
  • Every alert links back to the alerts archive entry captured at the time, wins and losses alike.

When the flow produces nothing for days

Stretches without observations usually mean the market is rotating inside wide CPR zones, where the rules deliberately restrain themselves. That restraint is observable: compare the quiet stretch against the historical analysis for similar regimes. Silence from the scanner is information about regime, not a malfunction — the failure mode to actually watch for is a STALE feed label, which the data gates already surface. If quiet stretches make you doubt the scanner, the observations journal lets you verify that similar regimes were equally quiet in the past — consistency across regimes is the audit trail.
