CPR Trading Guide for NIFTY & BANKNIFTY

The Central Pivot Range (CPR) is a floor-trader pivot framework built from the previous session's high, low and close. On TradingAI.in it forms the daily map for NIFTY and BANKNIFTY market classifications: where yesterday was undecided (the CPR zone), and where reactions are expected above (R1/R2) and below (S1/S2).

Educational guide only. CPR levels are analytical references, not personalised investment advice. Options involve substantial risk; see the Disclaimer.

1. CPR calculation (previous session only)

  • Pivot (H+L+C)/3; Bottom Central (BC) (H+L)/2; Top Central (TC) 2·Pivot − BC.
  • Width (TC−BC)/Pivot → narrow (<0.15%), normal, wide (>0.30%).
  • Floor levels: S1 2·P−H, R1 2·P−L, S2 P−range, R2 P+range, plus previous day high/low (PDH/PDL).
  • Always computed from the previous session — never from today or the future.

2. Narrow vs wide CPR days

  • Narrow CPR: indecision was tight — watch for breakout expansion beyond TC/BC.
  • Wide CPR: a broad undecided zone — price often rotates inside it; range-style behaviour.
  • TradingAI labels each day narrow/normal/wide and shows the zone on the 5-minute CPR chart.

3. Virgin CPR tests

A CPR is virgin (untested) when today's price has not yet touched the TC/BC zone. First tests of virgin levels are the highest-interest moments in this framework; once tested, the level's information value drops for the rest of the session.

4. Gaps and the first classified observation

  • Gap = today's open vs previous close: gap up (bullish), gap down (bearish), flat.
  • TradingAI classifies completed 5-minute candles from 09:15: downward-leaning observations at R2/R1/PDH touches or closes below BC; upward-leaning observations at S2/S1/PDL touches or closes above TC. The first classified observation of the day anchors the session context.
  • Reference observation point (next open, 09:20–15:20), reference bounds and session reference close are documented in the methodology.

6. CPR and the TradingAI signal flow

  • Levels first: each morning the previous session's high, low, and close produce Pivot, BC, TC, S1/S2, R1/R2, PDH, and PDL — the full reference map for the day.
  • Virgin tests: the first touch of an untested TC/BC zone in a completed 5-minute candle is the highest-interest event; the signal flow documents exactly which touches classify as upward- or downward-leaning observations.
  • First classified observation: gaps are measured at the open, then classification begins at 09:15. The first observation that meets the trigger rules anchors the session — everything before it is context, not signal.
  • Reference point and bounds: entries are recorded at the next open after a trigger (09:20–15:20 window), with reference bounds and a session reference close — all published in the methodology so any day can be rechecked.

7. CPR mistakes to avoid

  • Computing CPR from today: CPR is always previous-session data. Intraday highs and lows are for context; they never redraw the levels.
  • Treating CPR as a prediction: CPR marks where indecision and acceptance zones sit — it does not forecast which way price leaves them. The outlook label is a classification of current conditions, not a forecast.
  • Ignoring the gap: a large gap up into R2 behaves differently from a flat open inside a narrow CPR. Gap direction is one of the four published inputs — never read CPR levels without it.
  • Expecting wide days to trend: wide CPR days usually rotate inside the zone. Trend-style expectations belong to narrow-CPR breakouts, confirmed by acceptance (closes beyond TC/BC), not single wick touches.

8. Frequently asked questions

  • What does CPR stand for? Central Pivot Range — the zone between Top Central (TC) and Bottom Central (BC), computed from the previous session. It marks yesterday's zone of indecision carried into today.
  • Why only the previous session? Using settled, knowable data keeps the method reproducible: anyone can recompute any day's levels from public OHLC data and verify the classifications.
  • What is the difference between narrow and wide CPR? Narrow (width under ~0.15% of Pivot) means tight indecision — watch for breakout expansion. Wide (over ~0.30%) means a broad undecided zone — price often rotates inside it.
  • What is a virgin CPR test? The first touch of a TC/BC zone that today's price has not yet visited. First tests carry the most information; once tested, the level's value for the rest of the session drops.
  • Does price always respect CPR levels? No. Levels are reference zones where reactions are studied, not lines that hold. Breakouts, gap skips, and event days pass through them regularly — which is why exits and invalidation are part of the published rules.
  • Where can I watch CPR live? The NIFTY and BANKNIFTY outlook pages show the live CPR strip with gap and direction during market hours (09:15–15:30 IST, NSE weekdays).
