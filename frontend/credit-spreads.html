Bull Put Spread vs Bear Call Spread for NIFTY & BANKNIFTY

Researchers often study intraday contexts using credit vertical spreads: upward-leaning study contexts → Bull Put Spread, downward-leaning study contexts → Bear Call Spread. Premium is collected when a structure is opened and theoretical risk is bounded by the strike width. Nothing here suggests opening any structure on any day.

Illustrative educational examples with simplified premiums — not live prices and not personalised investment advice. Options involve substantial risk; see the Disclaimer.

3. Why credit spreads here

  • Defined risk: the long leg caps the loss at width minus credit.
  • Time decay helps: each passing day erodes option value, which favours the premium collector — small adverse drifts can still be tolerable, which is why the backtest uses decay-adjusted win labelling (see methodology).
  • Intraday only: no overnight gap risk; everything closes by 15:20.

4. Risks

  • Fast directional moves can reach the theoretical maximum loss; gaps can skip past spread levels.
  • Backtests use underlying/index moves as proxy, not historical option premiums — real fills, liquidity, brokerage and slippage differ (historical backtest).
  • Past hypothetical structures are recorded for study in historical model observations; they are not executed orders and suggest nothing.

5. Choosing between the two

  • Price holding above support (S1/S2/PDL) or accepting above TC: researchers study the bull put spread — the short put sits below price and time decay works for the position while support holds.
  • Price rejected at resistance (R1/R2/PDH) or accepting below BC: researchers study the bear call spread — the mirror image, with the short call above price.
  • Price inside a wide CPR with no trigger: neither structure is studied that day. Waiting for the first classified observation is itself the method (see signal flow).
  • Elevated India VIX days: credits are richer but moves are faster — the same widths carry the same point caps, reached more easily. Study size is reduced or the day is skipped.

7. Frequently asked questions

  • What is the difference between a bull put spread and a bear call spread? Direction. A bull put spread (puts, below price) is studied in upward-leaning contexts; a bear call spread (calls, above price) in downward-leaning ones. Both collect a credit and both cap loss at width minus credit.
  • Why study credit spreads instead of buying options outright? The long hedge leg defines the maximum loss in advance, and time decay works for the credit holder while price stays on the right side. An outright long option has unlimited (100%) premium loss and fights decay daily.
  • What happens if both legs expire worthless? That is the best case — the full net credit is retained, which is the theoretical maximum profit of either spread.
  • Can both legs end up losing? No — at expiry the spread value is bounded between zero and the width. The worst case is width minus credit, never more (European cash-settled index options).
  • Are the backtest results real profits? No. The historical backtest replays index moves through the spread rules hypothetically; it ignores historical option premiums, liquidity, brokerage, and slippage. Past hypothetical outcomes do not predict future results.
