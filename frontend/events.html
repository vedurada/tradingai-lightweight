How upcoming events affect a decision

The trading engine is rule-based: it qualifies a hypothetical observation only when the session is live, the latest completed five-minute candle is fresh, the scenario confirms and the risk rules pass. Scheduled events are layered on top as an advisory condition. When a high-impact event is imminent, the decision object raises an EVENT_RISK_HIGH flag, marks the setup as requiring an explicit confirmation, and shows the named event — but it does not silently block or reverse the setup.

Events with a release time after the 15:30 IST close (US FOMC, US CPI and payrolls, and India's CPI/WPI/GDP) are resolved to the next trading session, because that is when their effect reaches the NIFTY and BANKNIFTY open. Same-session events such as an RBI MPC decision or the Union Budget land during market hours.

Common questions

What are the market-moving events to watch?

RBI monetary policy decisions, US Federal Reserve FOMC decisions, India and US inflation prints, the Union Budget, quarterly GDP and monthly F&O expiry. Each is listed above with a date and an advisory impact label.

Do events change the model's signals?

No. Events annotate the decision and ask for confirmation; they never change entry, stop, target, direction or the risk and daily limits. See the methodology.

Why do some events affect the next session?

Releases after the NSE close (US Fed, US CPI/payrolls, India CPI/WPI/GDP) are felt at the next open, so the calendar resolves them to the next trading session.

Are all dates exact?

Central-bank and Budget dates are taken from primary sources. Monthly data-release dates and the exact F&O expiry weekday are approximate and marked as such — always confirm against the publisher.
