Expected Move Explained for NIFTY & BANKNIFTY Options

The Expected Move is the market-implied one-standard-deviation range for the underlying over a period — roughly, where options prices suggest the index could land about two-thirds of the time. TradingAI records it in options snapshots whenever the exchange feed is reachable.

Educational guide only. Metrics are analytical references, not personalised investment advice. Options involve substantial risk; see the Disclaimer.

1. What Expected Move is

  • Derived from at-the-money option premiums (straddle price): wider premiums imply a wider expected move.
  • It is a range of possibilities priced by the market, not a prediction that price will reach either edge.

2. Relationship with IV and time to expiry

  • Higher implied volatility (IV) widens the expected move; calm IV compresses it — see the India VIX guide.
  • Expected move shrinks as expiry approaches (less time left for movement) and resets on rollover to the next expiry.

3. How to interpret it

  • Compare the expected move with nearby CPR levels (CPR guide): a CPR support/resistance zone inside the expected move is ordinary rotation ground; levels far outside it need exceptional energy.
  • Useful for spread width and strike selection context (see credit spreads), not for picking direction.

4. Limitations

  • Real moves exceed one standard deviation roughly one session in three — breakouts past the expected move are normal, not anomalies.
  • Event days (policy, results, global shocks) regularly overshoot; IV can misprice jump risk.
  • Stale inputs produce stale ranges: always check data freshness, not just the number.
