Defined-Risk Option Spread Calculator for NIFTY and BANKNIFTY

A spread that combines two or four options has a loss decided when it is opened. You pay a premium and collect a premium; the difference between the two strikes caps how much can be lost. This page shows that arithmetic for spreads built from premiums you type in yourself, so you can study a structure before deciding whether it is worth your attention.

Educational material. It is not investment advice, a research recommendation, or an instruction to buy, sell, or hold any security or derivative.

Enter your prices

Every figure below is produced by the server-side calculation module. This page contains no pricing formula of its own — it sends what you type and displays the answer it receives.

Legs

Which leg is bought and which is sold is worked out by the calculation module from the strikes and option types you choose. You do not need to decide that yourself.

How to read the result

Maximum reward

The best outcome is the premium collected when the spread is a credit structure, or the difference between what you paid and the spread width when it is a debit structure. It is a fixed amount, not a percentage.

Maximum risk

For a credit spread this is the distance between the two strikes minus the premium collected. For a debit spread it is the premium paid. Either way the number is fixed when the position is opened, so the loss cannot grow beyond it while the position is open.

Breakeven

The index level at expiry where the profit and loss of the structure reaches zero. A credit spread has one breakeven level; an iron condor has two.

Estimated costs

Brokerage, charges, taxes and the spread between the two sides of the book, applied to the structure you entered. This is an estimate for study, not a contract statement.

The chart

Profit and loss at expiry across the plotted range. The flat sections at each end show the defined outcome: the capped loss on one side and the maximum reward on the other.

What this page does not do

  • It does not fetch live prices or an option chain. Everything comes from the premiums you enter.
  • It does not quote a probability, an expected outcome or a forecast for any index level.
  • It does not rank structures, suggest a strike, or compare instruments.
  • It does not place, size or record any position, and it stores nothing you type.
  • Premiums are treated as a mid-price fill, because you enter a single price per leg. Real fills sit between the best bid and the best offer.
