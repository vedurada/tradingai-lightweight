How to read today's status

The status card above shows one of three states. OPEN means the regular session is in progress right now (Monday–Friday, 9:15 AM–3:30 PM IST). PREMARKET means it is a trading weekday but before the 9:15 AM open — the pre-open auction may be running. CLOSED means no regular session today, with a short reason such as weekend, a scheduled holiday name, or outside session hours, plus the relevant date. The card refreshes from the live status feed; if the feed is unreachable it says so instead of guessing.

Beginners often confuse "market closed" with "data frozen": on holidays even the price cards on this site show their last session values with a closed timestamp. Always read the timestamp next to any price before treating it as live.

The pre-open call auction (9:00–9:15 AM)

Before the regular session, NSE runs a 15-minute call auction to discover a single orderly opening price. Roughly: the first minutes collect and modify orders, the middle minutes match them into an equilibrium opening price, and the final minutes transition into the continuous session. Orders placed here participate only in the opening match — unmatched orders typically flow into the regular book.

Why it matters for study: the opening price sets the day's first reference. A large gap between the previous close and the auction-discovered open often frames the whole session, which is why the CPR routine begins by assessing the 09:15 gap before anything else.

Why the market closes

Weekends. Saturday and Sunday are always non-sessions for NSE equities — no regular trading, no settlement activity to watch.

Scheduled holidays. NSE publishes an annual holiday circular (this page encodes circular CMTR71775 for 2026). Festivals such as Holi, Eid, Dussehra and Diwali-Balipratipada, plus national days, remove specific weekdays from the calendar. Exchanges occasionally declare additional holidays; the status card reflects the encoded list.

Special sessions. The Diwali Muhurat session is a short symbolic evening session, not a regular trading day — see below. It does not follow normal 9:15–15:30 hours.

Unforeseen closures. Extremely rare (system or city-wide events). On such days, treat every cached price as stale and wait for the exchange's own announcements.

What is Muhurat trading?

Muhurat is a roughly one-hour special equity session held on Diwali evening at an auspicious time announced by NSE each year. Participation is symbolic for many investors; volumes are thinner than a normal day and the session does not generate the standard daily range this site's models use. Do not compare Muhurat candles one-to-one with regular sessions when studying historical scenarios.

Using holidays well

Closed days are the best study days because there is no live price to distract you. A productive holiday routine: replay one past session in the historical scenario analysis, read one concept page from Learn (for example PCR or Max Pain), and journal what surprised you. The observations journal shows this same daily-logging discipline applied across sessions.

Common questions

Is the stock market open today?

The NSE trades Monday to Friday, 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM IST, except scheduled holidays. The status card above shows today's position.

What are NSE market timings?

Regular trading 9:15 AM–3:30 PM IST with a 9:00–9:15 AM pre-open auction. Learn how the open frames the day in the CPR guide.

What is the next NSE holiday?

See the dated table above. Holiday studying pairs well with the historical scenario analysis.

Does the market ever open on Saturday?

Regular NSE equity sessions never run on Saturday or Sunday. The only weekend trading most investors encounter is the Diwali Muhurat special session (usually a weekday evening, occasionally placed near a weekend) — it is symbolic, short, and not a regular session.

Why do option prices still show on holidays?

Cached snapshots from the last session remain visible with their timestamps. A price without a fresh timestamp is history, not a quote — this is why every TradingAI card carries a data timestamp and a live/delayed/closed feed label.
