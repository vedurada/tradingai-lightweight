Quick glossary

Plain definitions of the terms used across this site. Definitions only - none of these is a recommendation, and none is a prediction. For the full rulebook see methodology.

CPR (Central Pivot Range)
A band built from the previous session. TC (top of range) and BC (bottom of range) bracket where the prior session closed relative to the pivot. A narrow CPR suggests the prior session compressed into a small range; a wide CPR suggests it expanded. Read more in the CPR guide and narrow vs wide CPR.
R1, R2, S1, S2 (pivot levels)
Levels projected from the previous session's high, low and close. R1 and R2 sit above the pivot and act as potential resistance; S1 and S2 sit below it as potential support. This site uses them as reference levels inside its published rules - see support & resistance via CPR.
Support and resistance
Support is a price area where buyers have tended to step in; resistance is where sellers have tended to step in. They are reference areas, not guarantees, and they can fail.
VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)
The average price traded, weighted by volume, since the session started. Here it is used as a 5-minute cumulative typical-price reference to judge where price sits versus the day's average.
Regime
The prevailing market condition, such as trend or range. It describes the current state, not what may happen next. The labels this site can display are listed in methodology.
Open interest (OI)
The number of outstanding (unclosed) contracts in an option. OI shows how many positions are open, not how many will profit. Read more in the option chain guide.
PCR (Put-Call Ratio)
Total put open interest divided by total call open interest. Above 1 means more puts than calls are open; below 1 means the reverse. This site explains the concept but does not publish a live PCR value - see the PCR guide.
Max Pain
The price at expiry where option buyers collectively lose the most and option sellers collectively gain the most. This site explains the concept but does not publish a live Max Pain value - see the Max Pain guide.
India VIX
An index derived from NIFTY option prices that estimates the volatility the market expects over the next 30 days. Higher means a more expensive market. See the India VIX guide.
Spread (defined-risk structure)
Buying one option and selling another, so the maximum loss is capped. The two credit spreads explained here are the Bull Put Spread and the Bear Call Spread; compare them in credit spreads.
Break-even and maximum loss
Break-even is the price at which the structure's total profit and loss reach zero. Maximum loss is the worst outcome. You can work both out yourself in the fixed-loss options calculator using premiums you enter - it does not use live prices.
