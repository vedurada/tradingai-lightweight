How TradingAI works — a guide for new traders

TradingAI is a rules-based intraday decision aid for NIFTY, BANKNIFTY and INDIA VIX. It does not predict markets and it is not a tip service. It watches price structure all day, and only when a precise, pre-defined setup appears does it say TRADE — otherwise it says WAIT or NO TRADE. At most one setup per instrument per day.

The journey of a signal: previous session → CPR levels → 09:15 open → first trigger → entry at next open → stop/target working → exit by 15:10 → logged to Paper.

1. Market data — what the site sees

  • Prices come from a free intraday feed (yfinance) as 5-minute candles in India time (IST).
  • Only completed candles are used: at 10:04 the latest usable candle is the 09:55–10:00 one. The candle still forming is never used — a signal always comes from a closed candle, and entry happens at the next candle's open (09:20 at the earliest).
  • Stale quotes (older than the freshness limit) are labelled STALE; off-hours show MARKET CLOSED / WEEKEND / PREMARKET instead of pretending to be live.

2. CPR and levels — the map for the day

Every morning the previous session's high, low and close are converted into today's map. The central zone (CPR) is where yesterday was undecided; S1/S2 below and R1/R2 above are where reactions are expected:

  • Pivot (H+L+C)/3, BC (H+L)/2, TC 2·Pivot − BC; width (TC−BC)/Pivot → NARROW (<0.15%), NORMAL, WIDE (>0.30%). Narrow days chop; wide days run.
  • Floor pivots: R1 2·P−L, S1 2·P−H, R2 P+range, S2 P−range; plus PDH/PDL (previous day high/low).
  • Computed strictly from the previous session — never from today or the future.

3. How a strategy enters — the exact rules

  1. Scan: from the 09:15 candle, each completed candle is checked. Bearish triggers: high touches R2/R1/PDH, or close below BC. Bullish triggers: low touches S2/S1/PDL, or close above TC. The first trigger of the day wins (a same-candle tie goes to the bear side).
  2. Gate (default view): the trigger must agree with the weekly CPR — bulls only above weekly TC, bears only below weekly BC.
  3. Entry: at the next candle's open, between 09:20 and 15:10. No chasing, no market-on-close entries.
  4. Risk: 0.5% stop, 2% target, checked at subsequent opens (stop first). Anything still open exits at the 15:10 open — no overnight positions, ever.
  5. One per day: after entry the day is done for that instrument.
  6. Credit spreads only: every signal becomes a premium-collecting vertical — Bullish → Bull Put Spread, Bearish → Bear Call Spread (sideways markets take no spread; the Iron Condor exists only in the selection engine, not on the Paper page). Never debit spreads or naked options: option premiums decay every day (faster into expiry), so passing time pays a credit holder and punishes a premium buyer.
Worked example: NIFTY gaps below BC at 09:15 and closes there → BEAR/BELOW_CPR signal → short at the 09:20 open near 24,285 → price drifts to 23,883 by 15:10 → exit +401 points. If price had rallied through the 0.5% stop instead, the loss would be capped near −120 points. Winners ride the day's drift; losers are cut small — that asymmetry is the whole edge.

4. Reading the Home page

  • Price + change: live quote vs the previous trading day's close (not vs the last 5-minute bar).
  • Direction pill: BULLISH / BEARISH only when a setup qualified; MARKET CLOSED off-hours; AWAITING SETUP while scanning; DATA STALE when the feed lags during market hours.
  • 5-min CPR chart: today's completed candles with CPR lines and the live-price line.
  • Recommended Strategy + Decision detail: the engine's verdict with entry, stop, target and the evidence codes behind it.

5. Reading an instrument page (Nifty / BankNifty)

  • Bias & Regime: which side qualified and which market character drove it (continuation, range, breakout…). Empty on quiet days — that is information, not an error.
  • CPR strip + 5-minute chart + candle table (latest first), Gap (open vs prev close), Session timeline, and Why this decision with check-marked evidence codes.

6. Reading the Backtest page

  • Pick instrument, variant (Aligned = weekly gate, Plain = no filter) and dates → every historical trigger with entry/exit times, points, R and Win/Loss.
  • Win/Loss uses decay logic: tiny adverse drifts (under 30 NIFTY / 60 BANKNIFTY points) count as wins, because a short-option position's daily time-decay outweighs them. Full stops stay losses. Spot R is never rewritten.
  • Paper rows appended below the research rows (blue tint) are live logged days newer than your range — the table grows every session.

7. Reading the Paper page

  • Align cards + ledger: the same approved math, logged daily — one row per day per instrument. Totals here and on the Backtest page always agree.
  • Live Paper Spreads: when today triggers, the signal becomes a planned vertical spread (bear-call above spot / bull-put below, NIFTY 200pt / BANKNIFTY 400pt wide) on the true strike grid for the coming expiry — NIFTY weekly every Tuesday, BANKNIFTY monthly on the last Tuesday.
  • Payoff display: no live option premiums exist, so the page shows structure only — legs, width and expiry. No profit/loss curve, no max profit/loss, no breakeven is shown or implied anywhere.

8. Options spreads in 60 seconds (for novices)

  • A credit vertical spread sells one option and buys a further-out-of-the-money one: premium is collected on entry, risk is strictly defined, and cost is a fraction of outright options.
  • OTM short leg: the sold strike sits away from price, so small moves don't hurt — and each passing day's decay works for you, which is why sub-threshold drift days still count as wins here.
  • DTE matters: NIFTY weeklies (Tuesday) and BANKNIFTY monthlies (last Tuesday) are chosen so positions live inside healthy time-decay territory; nothing is held into expiry-day chaos.
  • Width & lot: 200pt (NIFTY) / 400pt (BANKNIFTY) between strikes; NSE lots 65 / 30 convert points to rupees.

9. How a trader benefits — and the risks

  • Benefit: a fixed plan replaces impulse — entries, stops, exits and position sizing decided before the market opens; small defined losses fund occasional big trend days; paper history shows every outcome, good and bad.
  • Risks: backtests exclude brokerage, taxes and slippage; short-premium positions can lose fast in gaps; past drift (e.g. a falling quarter) may not repeat. Never trade money you cannot lose.

10. Quick answers

  • Why NO TRADE today? No first trigger, a gated-out trigger, an incomplete feed, or the market is closed.
  • Why do two pages show different numbers? They shouldn't anymore — Backtest and Paper share one rulebook. The legacy qualification path (homepage strategy block) uses different levels by design.
  • What does FLAT / EOD / STOP / TARGET mean? How the trade ended: flat (no move), time exit at 15:10, stop hit, target hit.
