Option Chain & Open Interest Guide for NIFTY & BANKNIFTY

An option chain lists every strike's calls and puts with their open interest (OI), volume and premiums. For NIFTY and BANKNIFTY traders it is the positioning map: where the market has placed its biggest bets. TradingAI reads chain contracts through a validated data layer whenever the exchange feed is reachable.

Educational guide only. Metrics are analytical references, not personalised investment advice. Options involve substantial risk; see the Disclaimer.

1. Call OI, Put OI and change in OI

  • Open Interest = outstanding contracts not yet closed — a stock of positioning. Volume = contracts traded today — a flow.
  • Change in OI shows where positions were added today: rising OI with rising premium suggests fresh writing/buying conviction; rising OI with falling premium often means writers pressing.
  • Always read OI together with price: OI without price action is just inventory.

2. OI concentration

  • Concentration = a few strikes holding most of the chain's OI. Concentrated strikes act as gravitational zones into expiry.
  • Concentration that shifts intraday (writers defending a new strike) matters more than static totals.

3. Call concentration vs put concentration

  • Heavy call OI above spot often marks a supply zone (resistance); heavy put OI below spot often marks a demand zone (support).
  • These are positioning references for support & resistance, complementing CPR levels — not guarantees that levels will hold.

4. Support/resistance interpretation

  • Confluence counts: a CPR level (CPR guide) sitting on top of heavy OI concentration is a stronger reference than either alone.
  • Watch for OI unwinding (falling OI into a level break) — it often confirms the break better than the break itself.

5. Limitations

  • OI does not reveal direction of the holder (long vs short) or motive (hedge vs speculation).
  • Weekly-expiry rolls, event hedging and intraday squaring can redraw the map within hours.
  • Stale chains mislead: TradingAI validates contracts and marks freshness; unavailable data is shown as unavailable, never zero-filled.

7. How to read one chain row

Take a hypothetical NIFTY 24,800 row with spot at 24,820 (illustrative numbers): calls show LTP 95, OI 1.4 million, change-in-OI +300k, volume 2.1 million; puts show LTP 78, OI 1.1 million, change-in-OI +120k, volume 1.6 million. Reading it:

  • Premiums first: call 95 vs put 78 with spot just above the strike is ordinary — the ITM side costs more. Premiums are prices, not predictions.
  • Change-in-OI over OI: the +300k call build is the day's story (fresh positioning at this strike); the 1.4M total is background inventory. Big change on rising premium suggests buyers paying up; big change on falling premium suggests writers pressing.
  • Volume vs OI: volume far above change-in-OI means churn (positions opening and closing); volume near change-in-OI means genuine build-up. A 2.1M-volume / +300k-change row is mostly intraday turnover, not commitment.
  • Both sides together: calls building faster than puts at a strike above spot hints at supply interest there — one more reference to weigh alongside CPR levels, never a standalone verdict.

8. Chain-reading habits for expiry week

  • Track migration, not snapshots. Note which strikes gain OI across the week; writers defending a migrating strike telegraph where the battle line moved.
  • Discount Thursday afternoon. Expiry-day OI evaporates mechanically — compare morning structure, and read the day through price and the classification rules instead.
  • Separate hedge flow from bets. Steady two-sided OI growth into events is usually protection, not conviction; one-sided build with rising premium is the more opinionated flow.
  • Respect staleness. A chain that stopped updating mid-session is worse than no chain — TradingAI marks freshness explicitly for this reason, and you should demand the same of any source.

9. OI and the session clock

Morning OI changes show overnight positioning intentions; midday changes show writers adjusting to the developing range; late-session changes are dominated by squaring and expiry mechanics. The same +200k build means different things at 10:00 (conviction) and 15:00 (housekeeping). When comparing days, use same-time snapshots — morning-vs-morning, never morning-vs-close. And when the chain disagrees with price (heavy call writing while price grinds up through it), believe price: positioning describes where traders stood, while closes describe where the market is going. Make this your chain-reading motto: positioning is where traders stood, price is where the market is going.
