How a signal is generated

Deterministic, read-only evaluation on completed 5-minute candles. No lookahead, one trade per instrument per day. See methodology and CPR guide.

Trigger rule

BEAR only: first completed session close below SMA20 after the 20-period intraday SMA becomes available. Entry is the next candle open. At most one trade per instrument per session.

Entry and exit

  • Confirmation time is the timestamp of the completed candle whose close is below session SMA20.
  • Entry time is the following 5-minute candle open; the frozen signal keeps entry, stop and target fixed for that session.
  • The alerts archive, observations journal and historical analysis display the same active rule. Archived trades retain their original labels.

These examples use the current next-candle-open convention. Older records may show a different entry convention; their archived values are preserved as recorded and should be read under the rules active on that date.

Why each gate exists

  • Completed-candle confirmation. An intrabar dip below SMA20 is not enough; the close must confirm before entry.
  • Next-open execution. Entry at the following candle open avoids filling at a confirmation close that was not yet available.
  • Daily lock. One frozen signal per instrument and session prevents duplicate entries across API polls.
  • Fixed exits. The 0.5% stop, 2% target and 15:20 exit make spot-reference replay reproducible, but do not prove option-premium performance.

Reading WAIT vs NO TRADE

WAIT is provisional: the session is alive but no gate has produced an observation yet (no trigger, late read, momentary data gap). The scanner keeps evaluating each new 5-minute candle. NO TRADE is terminal for the day: a gate that cannot resolve before the close (holiday, session end with no trigger, persistent data failure). Days with no classified session are normal — particularly inside wide CPR rotation — and are recorded as such in the observations journal rather than hidden.

How to read a published alert

  • Direction + setup (BEAR SMA20_CLOSE_BELOW) identifies the completed close confirmation; the next 5-minute candle open is the entry.
  • Signal time is the trigger candle; entry time is the next candle's open — the same entry the backtest records for that trigger. The alert timestamp can follow the entry time because the engine evaluates completed candles and polls for updates.
  • Frozen trade: once fired, entry/stop/target never move for that session row — the journal studies the rule's outcome, not a managed position.
  • Every alert links back to the alerts archive entry captured at the time, wins and losses alike.

When the flow produces nothing for days

If no completed close below session SMA20 appears, the engine continues monitoring through the session and records no trade for that instrument. A STALE feed label means the engine cannot safely confirm a signal. Historical no-trade sessions remain visible in the observations journal.
