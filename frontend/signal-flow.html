How a signal is generated

Deterministic, read-only evaluation on completed 5-minute candles. No lookahead; every qualifying alignment is a separate signal. See methodology.

Trigger rule

BULL or BEAR: three consecutive completed 5-minute candles making higher highs, higher lows and higher closes, or lower highs, lower lows and lower closes. Entry is the next candle open. Every qualifying signal is kept; multiple trades per instrument per session are possible.

Entry and exit

  • Confirmation time is the timestamp of the third completed candle in the alignment.
  • Entry time is the following 5-minute candle open; the frozen signal keeps entry, stop and target fixed.
  • The alerts archive, observations journal and historical analysis display the same active rule. Archived trades retain their original labels.

These examples use the current next-candle-open convention. Older records may show a different entry convention; their archived values are preserved as recorded and should be read under the rules active on that date.

Why each gate exists

  • Completed-candle confirmation. An intrabar move is not enough; the third candle must close the alignment before entry.
  • Next-open execution. Entry at the following candle open avoids filling at an alignment close that was not yet available.
  • Per-signal identity. Each alignment has its own link and paper trade, so the same signal is never duplicated across API polls while later signals remain eligible.
  • Fixed exits. The 1.0-point buffer, 1.5R target and 15:25 exit make spot-reference replay reproducible, but do not prove option-premium performance.

Reading WAIT vs NO TRADE

WAIT is provisional: the session is alive but no completed alignment has formed yet. The scanner keeps evaluating each new 5-minute candle. NO TRADE is terminal for the day: a gate that cannot resolve before the close (holiday, session end with no alignment, persistent data failure). No-signal days are normal and are recorded in the observations journal rather than hidden.

How to read a published alert

  • Direction + setup (BULL H1_UP / BEAR H1_DOWN) identifies the completed three-candle alignment; the next 5-minute candle open is the entry.
  • Signal time is the third alignment candle; entry time is the next candle open — the same entry the backtest records. The alert timestamp can follow the entry time because the engine evaluates completed candles and polls for updates.
  • Frozen trade: once fired, entry/stop/target never move for that signal — the journal studies the rule's outcome, not a managed position.
  • Every alert links back to the alerts archive entry captured at the time, wins and losses alike.

When the flow produces nothing for days

If no three-candle alignment forms, the engine records no trade for that instrument. A STALE feed label means the engine cannot safely confirm a signal. Historical no-trade sessions remain visible in the observations journal.
