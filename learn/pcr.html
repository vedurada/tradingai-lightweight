PCR Explained: Put-Call Ratio

What PCR is

Total put open interest (or volume) divided by total call open interest. PCR above 1 means more put positioning than call positioning, and vice versa.

Reading it

Rising PCR with a rising market = hedged longs (healthy). PCR above ~1.3 often marks fear extremes that precede bounces; PCR below ~0.7 marks complacency that precedes falls.

OI vs volume PCR

OI-based PCR reflects positioning; volume PCR reflects today's urgency. TradingAI uses OI PCR for bias and volume PCR for confirmation.

Limits

PCR alone never triggers a trade on TradingAI — it is one vote alongside regime, VWAP, breadth and VIX.

How to read this page

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) lesson explains how derivative positioning can be read from options open interest. A higher PCR means puts are being accumulated relative to calls — often interpreted as caution — while a lower PCR points to call-heavy positioning. TradingAI computes PCR from the end-of-day options bhavcopy and shows it on the PCR page and index dashboards.

The article carefully explains the two common uses of PCR — as a contrarian sentiment gauge (extreme readings) and as a technical backdrop (open interest concentration) — and warns against a simple 'never call bottoms' rule. It also explains why the ratio varies by expiry and by instrument and why reading it involves the full option chain, not one number.

Combine the lesson with the live PCR page, which shows the ratio, max pain, and top open-interest strikes for NIFTY, BANKNIFTY and FINNIFTY, and with the option-chain lesson to understand the layout of puts and calls at each strike.

This is education, not tradeable advice. PCR is measured on close-of-day data, can lag the market, and is one input among many. Learn the concept, watch how it behaves across regimes, and never trade a single indicator.
